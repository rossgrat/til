# Making Learning Stick

Learning is tough but there have been studies on how to do it well.

The most effective, evidence based methods are:
1. Retrieval - Practice recalling things from memory, rather than re-reading or reviewing.
2. Spacing - Distribute learning over time.
3. Interleaving - Mix similar problem types so that you must pick an approach.
4. Feedback - Get delayed (rather than instant) corrective feedback. Spacing and retrieval without feedback entrenches errors.
5. Generation - Make an attempt before being taught, even if you're mostly wrong. Failed attempts prime encoding.
6. Elaboration - Ask "Why is this true?", "How does this connect to what I know?", out loud, in your own words.

The rest of these methods are somewhat effective as well:
7. Concrete examples, paired with an abstract rule
8. Dual coding, a verbal and visual representation of the same thing
9. Vary practice conditions
10. Metacognitive calibration - Treat your feeling of knowing as unreliable. Methods that feel productive produce the least durable learning, effective methods feel worse while you do them. "Desirable difficulties".
