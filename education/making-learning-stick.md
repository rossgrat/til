# Making Learning Stick

Learning is tough but there have been studies on how to do it well.

The most effective, evidence based methods are:
1. Retrieval - Practice recalling things from memory, rather than re-reading or reviewing.
2. Spacing - Distribute learning over time.
3. Interleaving - Mix similar problem types so that you must pick an approach.
4. Feedback - Get delayed (rather than instant) corrective feedback. Spacing and retrieval without feedback entrenches errors.
5. Generation - Make an attempt before being taught, even if you're mostly wrong. Failed attempts prime encoding.
6. Elaboration - Ask "Why is this true?", "How does this connect to what I know?", out loud, in your own words.

The rest of these methods are somewhat effective as well:
7. Concrete examples, paired with an abstract rule
8. Dual coding, a verbal and visual representation of the same thing
9. Vary practice conditions
10. Metacognitive calibration - Treat your feeling of knowing as unreliable. Methods that feel productive produce the least durable learning, effective methods feel worse while you do them. "Desirable difficulties".

## Sources
- [Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques (Dunlosky et al., 2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/) - Rated ten techniques by utility. Only practice testing and distributed practice earned "high"; interleaving, self-explanation, and elaborative interrogation landed at moderate.
- [Test-Enhanced Learning (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006)](http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PsychSci.pdf) - The retrieval result. Testing loses to re-reading on an immediate test and wins by a week out, which is why #1 feels wrong while you're doing it.
- [Teaching the Science of Learning (Weinstein, Madan & Sumeracki, 2018)](https://cognitiveresearchjournal.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s41235-017-0087-y) - Where #7 and #8 come from. Open access, and the most directly applicable of these.
- [Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way (Bjork & Bjork, 2011)](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf) - Origin of "desirable difficulties". The difficulty is the mechanism, not a side effect.
- [Learning versus Performance (Soderstrom & Bjork, 2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25910388/) - Backs #10. Performance during practice is a poor proxy for durable learning, which is what makes self-assessment untrustworthy.
